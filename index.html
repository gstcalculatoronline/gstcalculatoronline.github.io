What is Goods and Services Tax (GST)?

The Goods and Services Tax (GST) is a comprehensive, multi-stage, destination-based indirect tax imposed on the supply of goods and services. Implemented to replace a fragmented system of cascading taxes (such as VAT, service tax, excise duty, and luxury tax), GST unifies national market valuations under standardized rates.

Key Tax Components: CGST, SGST, and IGST Explained

Under the Indian GST framework, taxes are split depending on whether the trade transaction is intrastate (within the same state) or interstate (between two different states):

  • CGST (Central Goods and Services Tax): Collected by the Central Government on intrastate sales.
  • SGST (State Goods and Services Tax): Collected by the respective State Government on intrastate sales.
  • IGST (Integrated Goods and Services Tax): Levied by the Central Government on interstate transactions and imports, then distributed accordingly between the Union and receiving State.

When computing taxes for local sales, the total GST percentage is split equally between CGST and SGST (for instance, an 18% slab rate divides into 9% CGST and 9% SGST).

Standard GST Tax Slabs

Goods and services are categorized under four primary rate slabs alongside essential exempt items:

  • 5% Rate: Applied to essential consumer goods, basic foods, apparel under specific caps, and mass-market services.
  • 12% Rate: Applicable to processed food items, business equipment, computers, and specific agricultural machinery.
  • 18% Rate: The default slab covering the majority of consumer items, IT services, financial services, and industrial products.
  • 28% Rate: Applied to luxury goods, automobiles, sin goods (tobacco, sugary beverages), and high-end entertainment.

How to Calculate GST: Exclusive vs. Inclusive Tax

Our online utility accommodates both major financial calculation modes:

1. Adding GST to Base Amount (Exclusive GST)

To determine the total gross price when tax is added on top of a net cost, use the following formula:

$$\text{GST Amount} = \frac{\text{Net Amount} \times \text{GST Rate}}{100}$$

$$\text{Gross Amount} = \text{Net Amount} + \text{GST Amount}$$

2. Extracting GST from Total Amount (Inclusive GST)

When the tax is already integrated into the final retail sticker price, calculate the original base price and tax portion using:

$$\text{GST Amount} = \text{Gross Amount} - \left( \frac{\text{Gross Amount} \times 100}{100 + \text{GST Rate}} \right)$$

$$\text{Net Amount} = \text{Gross Amount} - \text{GST Amount}$$
